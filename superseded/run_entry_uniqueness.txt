  sigma    eta      frontier (this model)   frontier (sharper notion)

  5.0      1.5      0.936                   0.652

  8.0      1.0      0.532                   0.524
  3.0      1.0      0.572                   0.540

  READING. Concavity holds everywhere; it is unconditional. Strategic
  substitutability does not: past a share of about one half, Cournot
  profit is convex enough in the share that a parent's marginal value
  of capability RISES as the market aggregate grows. So the theorem carries a
  hypothesis with real content -- NO SINGLE PARENT HOLDS MORE THAN
  ABOUT HALF OF A MARKET -- rather than being free. It is checked at
  the solution, market by market, not assumed. The frontier is a
  SUFFICIENT condition: PART 3 finds uniqueness beyond it too.

==============================================================================
PART 3   IS THE ENTRY EQUILIBRIUM UNIQUE, WITH INTERNALISATION?
==============================================================================
Brute force over EVERY configuration -- each parent choosing any subset of
its own affiliates -- keeping the Nash ones, where a deviation is scored on
the RE-SOLVED market equilibrium. This is the case Gaubert-Itskhoki's
cutoff cannot handle and Yang resolves by imposing an order.

  markets with a pure-strategy entry equilibrium  : 120 / 120
    of those, UNIQUE                              : 120
    of those, multiple                            : 0
  solver returned an answer                       : 120
  solver issued its uniqueness CERTIFICATE        : 120
  certificate issued where brute force found many : 0   <- must be 0

  The cheap solve uses the model's entry condition (aggregate taken as
  given). Brute force uses the sharper one (every deviation re-solves the
  market). How far apart are they?
    cheap answer already an exact Nash equilibrium : 82 / 120
    cheap answer survives nash_refine untouched    : 82 / 120
    after one refinement it is an exact Nash eq.   : 120 / 120

  READING. Brute force is the referee: it enumerates every configuration
  with parents internalising and checks each deviation on the re-solved
  equilibrium. It finds ONE equilibrium every time -- which is the claim
  that matters, and the claim the old order-selection device was there to
  paper over. The certificate is what the model can carry into a GE loop,
  where brute force is not affordable.

==============================================================================
PART 4   IS THE BEST RESPONSE MONOTONE IN THE AGGREGATE?
==============================================================================
K*(A) must be non-increasing for every parent, and sum_g S_g(A) decreasing.
Tested on the exact discrete subset problem the solver solves.

  K*(A) NOT non-increasing, over the whole grid : 193 / 1200  (worst rise +3.23e+01)
  ... restricted to shares inside the frontier  : 0 / 1200
  markets where sum_g S_g(A) is NOT decreasing   : 0 / 200

  The grid runs over six orders of magnitude in A, so most of it sits far
  outside anything the model visits: at the low end a single parent owns
  the market outright, which is exactly where condition B lapses. Inside
  the frontier the monotonicity is clean, and the object the solver
  actually relies on -- the clearing function -- is decreasing everywhere.

  Compare with the earlier pass, which found the Gaubert-Itskhoki condition
  violated in 226/300 cases once parents internalised. That condition mixes
  a firm's own effect with its rivals'. Separated, the own effect is
  concavity and the rival effect is substitutability, and each behaves.
