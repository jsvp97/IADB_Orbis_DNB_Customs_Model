PART 1  THE MARKUP CHANNEL, IN CLOSED FORM
============================================================================
  A group's factor bill moves with E S / mu(S); the between-group term of
  LEMMA 4 has the gross-substitutes sign exactly when eps_mu(S) <= 1.
  Closed form, Cournot eta = 1:  mu = sigma/[(sigma-1)(1-S)], so
  eps_mu = S/(1-S) < 1  <=>  S < 1/2. THE SAME HALF AS THEOREM 2.

  between the highest and lowest relative wage -- is at most 2R implies at

PART 4  THE COUNTEREXAMPLE: GROSS SUBSTITUTES IS NOT A THEOREM HERE
============================================================================
  Three countries, one sector. Country 1 has local firms. Country 2 owns
  parents whose ONLY plants are in country 3, so every euro country 2 earns
  is a head-office payment for production abroad. Raise w_3: those parents
  get dearer, lose share to country 1, and country 2's wage bill FALLS.
  Every design below has enough local rivals that NO parent holds more than
  a third of any market, so (H1) is satisfied throughout and cannot be what
  fails. The maxS column is the largest parent share over ALL markets.

  design                                            M[3,2] min offdg  H2 slack    maxS   index
   14 rivals in 1, 10 in 3, alpha 0.15, 3 parents   -0.2192   -1.4288   -1.4288   0.177      +1
   10 rivals in 1,  6 in 3, alpha 0.30, 2 parents   -0.3096   -0.8270   -0.8270   0.248      +1
   10 rivals in 1,  6 in 3, alpha 0.50, 2 parents   -0.3546   -0.3920   -0.4482   0.293      +1
   10 rivals in 1,  6 in 3, alpha 0.80, 2 parents   +0.0975   +0.0565   -0.3362   0.308      +1
    6 rivals in 1,  3 in 3, alpha 0.30, 2 parents   -0.2694   -0.6344   -0.6344   0.291      +1
   10 rivals in 1,  6 in 3, alpha 0.30, Bertrand    -0.5030   -1.2263   -1.2263   0.325      +1
   10 rivals in 1,  6 in 3, alpha 0.30, nu = 0.55   -0.1145   -0.5116   -0.5116   0.227      +1

  READING. The largest parent share anywhere is 0.33, so (H1) is satisfied at
  every one of these -- and gross substitutes fails at six of the seven. It is
  (H2), the head-office exposure condition, that is doing the work, and the
  counterexample is what proves it cannot be dropped.

  Note the alpha pattern, which is the opposite of the obvious guess: the
  failure is WORST at alpha = 0.15 and DISAPPEARS by alpha = 0.80. A large
  head-office share is not the problem. The problem is a country whose whole
  income is a thin slice of firms whose costs are set by one other country.

PART 6  WHERE GROSS SUBSTITUTES FAILS, IS THE EQUILIBRIUM STILL UNIQUE?
============================================================================
  Gross substitutes is sufficient, not necessary. The weaker requirement is
  that every equilibrium have INDEX +1 -- sign det of the reduced Jacobian
  equal to (-1)^(N-1). Walras' law makes the wage bills the left null vector
  of I - M, so every diagonal cofactor of I - M is proportional to a wage
  bill: THE INDEX DOES NOT DEPEND ON WHICH COUNTRY IS THE NUMERAIRE. Below,
  random economies from the family in PART 4, each solved from 25 dispersed
  starts.

  economies solved                                    : 50
  gross substitutes FAILS at the equilibrium in       : 34
  index differs from +1 in                            : 0
  index depends on the choice of numeraire in         : 0
  MULTIPLE equilibria found in                        : 0

  So the failure of gross substitutes is real but it is not fatal: the
  index condition, which is all uniqueness actually needs, survives it.
  Theorem 4 proves the sufficient condition; this is the evidence that the
  necessary one is much weaker, and it is the honest limit of the result.
