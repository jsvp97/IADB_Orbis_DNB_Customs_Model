PART A   ONE MARKET, AND WHY IT HAS EXACTLY ONE SOLUTION  [cournot]
==============================================================================
Cannibalisation is the mechanism: a parent's own varieties are perfect
substitutes inside its own output aggregate, so expanding one depresses
the others and the markup rises with the PARENT's total share. The same
object mu(S_g) appears under quantity and price competition, which is why
the solver takes mu as a parameter.

  conduct     kappa     mu(0)       mu(0.5)     share frontier
  cournot     4.000     1.250       2.500       0.548
  bertrand    0.800     1.250       1.500       0.680

  kappa is the coefficient on the S^2 term in profit, so the ownership
  correction is proportional to it. Conduct changes the MAGNITUDE by a
  factor of five and changes nothing structural.

PART B   ENTRY: IS THE EQUILIBRIUM UNIQUE, WITH INTERNALISATION?  [cournot]
==============================================================================
Brute force over EVERY configuration -- each parent free to choose any
subset of its own plants -- keeping the Nash ones, where a deviation is
scored on the RE-SOLVED market. This is the case Gaubert-Itskhoki's cutoff
cannot handle and Yang resolves by imposing an entry order.

  markets with a pure-strategy entry equilibrium  : 40 / 40
    of those, UNIQUE                              : 40
    of those, multiple                            : 0
  solver issued its uniqueness CERTIFICATE        : 40 / 40
  certificate issued where brute force found many : 0   <- must be 0
  cheap solve already an exact Nash equilibrium   : 28 / 40
  cheap solve survives nash_refine untouched      : 28 / 40

  The certificate is what travels into the GE loop, where brute force is
  unaffordable: the solver re-checks that the clearing function is
  decreasing with exactly one sign change, market by market.

The two conditions the theorem rests on
---------------------------------------
(A) Omega concave in own K   -> the best response is a cutoff on the
    parent's OWN list, so internalisation is not an obstacle;
(B) Omega_K falling in A     -> strategic substitutes.
Both by numerical differentiation of Omega, using none of the algebra.

  (A) concavity  violated   0 / 150 over all shares, 0 / 112 inside the frontier
  (B) substitutes violated  38 / 150 over all shares, 0 / 112 inside the frontier


  These are not assumptions. For Cournot with eta = 1 they have CLOSED
  FORMS, and the closed forms are checked against the numbers above:

  closed form vs numerical differentiation      : 4.35e-05
  (A) violated at any share, sigma in 2..12     : 0
  (B) violated at any share <= 1/2              : 0
  slack at S = 1/2 equals 1/sigma, error        : 0.00e+00
  'no parent above 1/2' fails for other eta or Bertrand : 0
  worst slack across those cases                : 0.125

  (A) is proved at EVERY share. (B) is proved at every share <= 1/2, with
  slack exactly 1/sigma at the boundary. So the theorem's hypothesis is
  "no parent holds more than half of any market" -- an economically
  meaningful restriction that can be checked in the data, not a technical
  assumption. It is sufficient, not necessary: uniqueness is found beyond it.

The solver's own map is a contraction -- uniqueness, constructively
-------------------------------------------------------------------
  Theorems 4 and 5 rule out a second equilibrium with a condition imported
  from outside. This is the direct statement: the map the solver iterates,
  w <- w (LD/L)^kappa, is homogeneous of degree one, its log-Jacobian
  (1-kappa)I + kappa M has unit row sums by that homogeneity, and where it
  is entrywise POSITIVE Birkhoff's theorem makes it a contraction in
  Hilbert's projective metric. One contraction, one fixed point, and the
  iteration converges to it from anywhere.

  spread     pts      min M   min M_nn max damping   min A_k  Birkhoff
  0.00         1    +0.0221    +0.0221     1.0226   +0.0515    0.8191
  0.40        10    +0.0065    +0.0065     1.0065   +0.0458    0.8295
  0.80        10    -0.0049    -0.0049     0.9951   +0.0409    0.8535
  direct check on 12 pairs: worst observed ratio 0.6991 against the
  certified bound 0.8485 -- bound respected: yes
  The damping is not a numerical convenience: where labour demand is
  elastic the UNDAMPED map is not monotone at all, and the model itself says
  how much damping is needed. Here the largest admissible kappa is 0.995
  and the solver uses 0.25. At the calibrated five-country economy the
  requirement is 0.402 (quantities) and 0.390 (prices); with the
  input-output block off it tightens to 0.273, where 0.15 would be safer.

Uniqueness: same wages AND the same set of firms, from every start
------------------------------------------------------------------

  cannot make, and the one that did not exist before entry: wages could
  agree while a DIFFERENT SET OF FIRMS operated, which is the multiplicity
  the entry order was invented to paper over.

PART D   THE SIX STYLIZED FACTS  [cournot]
==============================================================================
  potential plant-destination pairs : 608;  active 512 (84.2%);  unresolved 2

FACT 1  multinationals are a large share of export value, and FOREIGN ones dominate
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  model  total 0.631 = foreign 0.560 + domestic 0.071
  data   total 0.46-0.74, foreign dominant, domestic 0.02-0.28
  The LEVEL is one fitted parameter. The SPLIT is not: it comes from the
  pool of potential parents scaling with country size, so poor countries
  rarely breed a parent good enough to clear the multinational threshold.
  With a uniform pool (zeta = 0) the model puts domestic at 0.245 and
  contradicts the fact outright.

FACT 2  foreign multinationals specialise in COMPLEX goods, domestic ones in simple
-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  model foreign  by sector: 0.33 0.70 0.65 0.66   gradient +0.63
  model domestic by sector: 0.10 0.14 0.00 0.06   gradient -0.16
  data  foreign  0.52 0.52 0.63 0.65 0.70   gradient +0.43
  data  domestic 0.12 0.02 0.06 0.03 0.02   gradient negative
  The FOREIGN half is reproduced and is the one that matters: it comes from
  hq_gap, the statement that headquarter services are a capability a
  stand-alone local firm cannot buy, and that complex goods need them most.
  Without hq_gap the model gets it BACKWARDS (gradient -0.58), because a
  foreign affiliate pays its parent's high wage on the alpha_k share and
  the extensive margin amplifies that wrong-sign force.
  THE DOMESTIC HALF IS NOT ROBUST and is reported, not claimed: it comes out
  NEGATIVE in a four-country world and POSITIVE in a five-country one. A
  domestic multinational is a LAC parent, so it escapes the hq_gap penalty
  while still paying a low home wage on the alpha share -- the wrong-sign
  force again, now working in its favour. The model pins down that domestic
  multinationals are FEW (Fact 1), not where they specialise. Open item.

FACT 3  parents come from a small set of countries -- GENERATED
---------------------------------------------------------------
  model parent-country HHI 0.396, top parent 0.525
  data                0.130,            0.250
  Nothing tells the model which countries host parents: the capability law
  is identical everywhere. Concentration comes from a bigger pool having a
  better maximum. It overshoots in a small world, as it must.

FACT 4  grouping plants by parent RAISES measured concentration
---------------------------------------------------------------
  model  0.121 -> 0.121 -> 0.182   (x1.50)
  data   0.192 -> 0.209 -> 0.215   (x1.12)
  ORDERING REPRODUCED. It is a PREDICTION, not a target, and it is the reason
  internalisation had to survive the entry rewrite: the whole uniqueness
  theorem exists so that this fact could.
  parents operating in >=2 countries: 14% of parents, 68% of value
  data: parents with >100 affiliates are 9% of parents and 56% of value
  The direction is right. The LEVEL is not comparable: the model's network
  size runs 1..N countries, the data's runs 1..100+ affiliates worldwide.

FACT 5  more multinationals, MORE exports by local firms -- THE ONE FAILURE
---------------------------------------------------------------------------
  non-MNE export value in sector 2: 0.0484 -> 0.0016   change -96.7%
  data: POSITIVE. Saturated specification, +0.24 (intensive), +0.13 (extensive)
  STILL NEGATIVE. Business stealing operates on the ENTRY margin too
  and dominates the cheaper-inputs channel, so entry makes this fact WORSE.
  Two candidate resolutions, and they are testable against each other:
   (i) a genuine Javorcik spillover (the `spill` knob), which must now be
       LARGER than the 0.15 the fixed-roster model needed; or
  (ii) the empirical coefficient is contaminated: the fixed-effect ladder
       has no destination x product x year term, so a market-level demand
       shock raises multinational entry and local exports together.
  Adding that fixed effect is the decisive test and it is cheap.

FACT 6  distance is a weaker barrier for multinationals
-------------------------------------------------------
  ln distance                       -0.558      data -0.164
  ln distance x MNE                 +0.909      data +0.046
  ln distance x MNE, present there  +0.455      data +0.067
  Falls out of d[l,n] running from the PRODUCTION country -- Tintelnot's
  export platforms. Signs and ordering right; magnitudes too large, and
  note the data's own -0.164 is an order of magnitude below any gravity
  estimate, which is a specification smell on the empirical side.
